What does a useful employee noise exposure note communicate?

The employee and client need to understand the result, the exposure limit used for comparison and whether the result is above or below that limit. Identifying the selected agency keeps the basis of the comparison clear.

myIH generates employee exposure notes for noise with one agency comparison per sample. The consultant selects from the available statutory and non-statutory agencies to match the assessment’s comparison criteria.

How is an agency selected for each noise sample?

myIH supports statutory and non-statutory agencies, with one selected agency used for each noise sample’s comparison. The exposure note communicates the result against that selection.

Employee notification PDFs use green, yellow and red color-coding for exposure-limit comparisons. Keeping the agency identified helps the consultant explain what the comparison means to the employee or client.

The selected criterion should reflect the assessment’s purpose. Clearly identifying a statutory limit or non-statutory guideline keeps that context attached to the result.

How does myIH keep noise observations connected to the employee?

Noise monitoring takes place during a working day that can include several tasks and locations. Employee observations help explain what happened during the measurement period and make the record more useful when it is reviewed later.

myIH’s daily log supports employee observations and instrument/calibration device checks. These entries appear alongside sample information and surrounding context in the workflow. The consultant can review the monitoring record with the events and checks documented during the survey.

Illustrative example: “09:40 — Employee assigned to NOISE-008 left the press area for tool setup. Returned at 10:15.” A time-stamped observation gives the reviewer a specific work transition to consider alongside the measurement record.

How does this improve the handoff from survey to employee communication?

myIH keeps the employee’s monitoring information, field context and exposure documentation within one IH workflow. The consultant can prepare an employee exposure note without rebuilding the surrounding record from separate field notes and spreadsheets.

“PPE Used” is included on employee exposure PDFs. Keeping that information in the exposure record helps retain the details of the employee’s working conditions during the assignment.

The resulting PDF can be shared and retained with the project documentation. If the client stores employee records in another EHS system, the exported PDF can be manually placed in that system’s document repository.

For a consultancy, the value is a clearer deliverable: an employee-specific noise exposure note that communicates the selected agency comparison and is supported by the field record.

How do noise results roll up to the client dashboard?

Noise results roll up to myIH’s client dashboard, where samples are organized into OEL bands by sample percentage of the selected OEL. This gives the IH team a client-level view of how the recorded samples compare with their exposure limits.

The dashboard brings those sample comparisons together for review and discussion. The employee exposure note retains the specific agency comparison behind the individual sample.

myIH also provides client access, allowing IH teams to share results directly with their clients. The consultancy can use the dashboard to discuss the overall picture and individual exposure records to explain the detail, with PDF exports available for document sharing and recordkeeping.
