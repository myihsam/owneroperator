How can IH consultants standardize field notes across a team?

Use myIH prompted questions, daily logs and remote visibility to improve field documentation, guide junior hygienists and simplify report preparation.

What does consistent field documentation give a consultancy?

A senior hygienist should be able to understand a field record without first interviewing the person who collected it. That requires more than consistent formatting. The record needs the observations that explain the task, employee, sampling conditions and events during the visit.

myIH’s prompted questions give the team a shared approach to capturing those details. For recurring projects, the consultancy can organize questions around the work being evaluated so each field hygienist addresses the information needed for review.

How do prompted questions carry experienced judgment into fieldwork?

Start with the questions your senior CIH regularly asks when reviewing a job. For a transfer operation, that may mean documenting which activities occurred, whether a release was observed, the controls present and any sampling interruptions. For welding, the process, metal, electrode and work environment provide specific context for the assessment.

Use the questions to focus attention at the time of collection. A prompt about an interruption is most useful while the hygienist can check what happened, speak with the employee and record the time.

Just “PPE used?” That is a useful question, and myIH includes “PPE Used” on employee exposure PDFs. Where relevant, add context about the task and the protection observed so the reviewer understands the circumstances.

How does the daily log complement prompted questions?

Prompts establish the details the team expects to collect. The daily log records instrument/calibration device checks and employee observations as the day develops. It gives the hygienist a place to capture a task change, equipment check or other event that needs a time and an explanation.

Question responses and log entries appear alongside sample information and surrounding context in the workflow. A reviewer can follow the connection between what was sampled and what was happening around that sample.

Illustrative example: a prompted response records the welding process, while an employee-linked log entry records a move to a different work area during the monitoring period. Together, they give the reviewer a more useful account than either detail alone.

How can CIHs support junior hygienists remotely?

myIH gives CIHs and managers real-time access to field data and observations. They can review what the team is capturing, identify details that need clarification and provide guidance without being at every job site.

For junior staff, that makes documentation a practical teaching opportunity. A senior hygienist can explain why the timing of a task change matters or why a note needs to identify the employee concerned. The guidance relates to the actual work in progress, when the field team can still gather the missing context.

How does this reduce work at report time?

Consistent collection gives the report writer a familiar record to work from. Connected observations reduce the need to chase separate notebooks, reinterpret shorthand or ask the team to remember an equipment check days later.

After a project, use the questions that came up during review to improve the next set of prompts. That creates a repeatable mentoring practice: the consultancy’s experience shapes collection, and field experience improves the next job’s documentation.
